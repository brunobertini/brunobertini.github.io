I study coherent quantum many-body systems in highly excited non-equilibrium states, i.e. "non-equilibrium quantum matter". These systems occur in Nature throughout a wide range of different scales, from the astronomical ones to those of semiconductor devices, and their dynamics are typically dominated by the interactions. Fundamental theoretical questions concern understanding the general principles governing the on-set of ergodicity and the emergence of classical behaviours at late times, determining the general laws governing the spreading and scrambling of quantum information, classifying new dynamical phases occurring out of equilibrium, and characterising the effects of quantum noise or monitoring through measurements.

The systematic study of these questions has begun with the new millennium, when a number of critical breakthroughs made it possible to realise non-equilibrium quantum matter in real experiments, and received a huge boost in the last five years, with the advent of digital quantum simulators (or noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices). With the huge wave of activity triggered by the arrival of these devices, and the great collective strive to further improve them and achieve fault tolerant quantum computers, the study of quantum matter out of equilibrium has now well and truly entered a golden era. 

Here is a brief list of my main research directions

* Out-of-equilibrium Dynamics in Bethe-Ansatz Integrable Models

* Exactly Solvable Interacting Minimal Models of Many-Body Dynamics 

* Quantum Many-Body Chaos 

* Emergent Space-Time Symmetries and Space-Time Duality 


Here is a brief list of my main achievements (citations refer to the publication list):

* Development of generalized hydrodynamics (GHD), a hydrodynamic theory describing the large- scale asymptotics of systems with macroscopically many local conservation laws. GHD has led to many theoretical developments in integrability, fluid theory, statistical mechanics, and, concurrently, has found direct application in cold-atom experiments. Based on its impact on the scientific community (it has been the main subject of several international conferences, lecture courses, reviews, and even popular science articles), it is fair to say that GHD constitutes one of the most influential achievements in quantum integrability during the last three decades.

* Discovery and characterisation of dual-unitary quantum circuits, a class of quantum many-body systems that are simultaneously chaotic and exactly solvable. My results on dual- unitary circuits have showed that it is possible to describe detailed properties of chaotic quantum many-body systems in a mathematically rigorous manner. This extraordinary achievement has led to substantial developments in quantum dynamics, quantum chaos, quantum information, and quantum computation that are still vigorously ongoing. They also led to practical developments as they are currently used as a benchmark for testing real quantum computers.

* First exact results on the spectral statistics of interacting quantum many-body systems, and first test of the quantum chaos conjecture away from the semiclassical regime.

* Development of the space-time duality approach, based on the idea of describing the dynamics of a many-body system (hard) by computing the equilibrium properties (easier) of the dual system where the roles of space and time are exchanged. This has led to a simple explanation of the ubiquitous linear growth of entanglement after quantum quenches and to exact formulas describing entanglement growth, charge fluctuations, and symmetry restoration in integrable systems.

* Theoretical description of prethermalization, an integrable-to-non-integrable crossover similar to that described by the Kolmogorov-Arnold-Moser theory in few-particle classical systems. This phenomenon is of crucial practical importance: it shows that integrability can be experimentally observed.
